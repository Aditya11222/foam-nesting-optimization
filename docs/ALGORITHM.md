# 3D Foam Block Nesting & Cutting Optimization: Guillotine Slicing Algorithm

## 1. Executive Summary & Plain Language Overview

Polyurethane foam manufacturing begins with large, expensive rectangular foam blocks (known in the industry as **"buns"**, e.g., 108" × 82" × 40"). These buns must be converted into high-mix, variable-size rectangular parts (cushions, bolsters, cubes, pads, mattresses) with minimum material waste.

Because foam cutting on the shop floor relies primarily on **guillotine and band saws** (vertical and horizontal saws), cutting is physically restricted: **every cut must slice all the way through the workpiece from edge to edge (or face to face)**. A saw cannot turn 90 degrees inside the foam block or carve out an internal pocket without slicing through the entire block.

This document describes the 3D guillotine nesting algorithm designed to solve this problem while honoring all physical manufacturing constraints, material anisotropy (foam rise direction), skin trim, kerf loss, and inventory remnants.

> **Honesty & Transparency Rule:**
> 3D guillotine cutting-stock problems are mathematically NP-hard. All layout solutions produced by this engine are explicitly designated as **"heuristic solutions"**, and utilization metrics are reported transparently against both gross and usable block volumes without claiming global optimality.

## 2. Physical & Manufacturing Rules Enforced

Every layout generated by the algorithm strictly complies with the following domain rules:

### 2.1 Units & Dimension Snapping (1/16")
- All dimensions (length, width, height, coordinates, kerf, trim) are measured in **inches**.
- To prevent floating-point accumulation drift and match saw operator scale increments, all inputs and calculated cut coordinates snap strictly to increments of **1/16 inch** (`0.0625"`):
  $$\text{snapped\_val} = \frac{\text{round}(v \times 16)}{16}$$
- Any part dimension with sub-1/16" fractional precision that cannot be cleanly snapped or falls below 1/16" is rejected with an explanatory error.

### 2.2 Mandatory Skin Trim (~1" Per Face)
- When a raw foam bun is poured and cured, its outer surfaces develop a hard, oxidized, irregular skin layer.
- By default, **1.0 inch** of skin must be trimmed off all 6 outer faces of every raw bun.
- The **usable volume** for part placement is therefore strictly:
  $$L_{\text{usable}} = L_{\text{nominal}} - 2 \times \text{trim}_X$$
  $$W_{\text{usable}} = W_{\text{nominal}} - 2 \times \text{trim}_Y$$
  $$H_{\text{usable}} = H_{\text{nominal}} - 2 \times \text{trim}_Z$$
- Parts are placed **exclusively** inside this usable bounding box. Skin trim cannot be used for customer parts.
- Any raw block whose usable dimension is $\le 0$ is rejected.

### 2.3 Saw Kerf Allowance (1/8")
- Every cut executed on a saw removes a thin slice of foam material turned into dust/shavings, known as the **kerf**.
- Default kerf for guillotine band saws is **1/8 inch** (`0.125"`), configurable per machine profile.
- Whenever a parent block is divided along any cut plane into two child pieces, the kerf thickness is consumed between them:
  $$\text{Dimension}_{\text{parent}} = \text{Dimension}_{\text{child1}} + \text{Dimension}_{\text{child2}} + \text{kerf}$$

### 2.4 Foam Anisotropy & Rise Direction (Z Axis)
- Flexible polyurethane foam expands vertically during the chemical foaming reaction. This vertical expansion axis is called the **rise direction** (designated as the **Z axis**).
- Foam has anisotropic mechanical properties (compression modulus, firmness, cell structure) along its rise axis compared to lateral axes.
- Each part SKU defines a **permitted-orientation rule**:
  1. **`rise-locked` (Default):** The part's height dimension must remain aligned with the block's Z axis. The part may only be rotated $0^\circ$ or $90^\circ$ in the horizontal XY plane (up to 2 valid orientations).
  2. **`free-in-plane`:** The part must keep its Z axis aligned with the block Z axis, but can freely rotate by $90^\circ$ increments in the XY plane.
  3. **`any`:** The part can be oriented along any of the 6 canonical 3D Cartesian orientations ($X, Y, Z$ permutations).
- **Duplicate collapsing:** If two or three dimensions of a part are identical (e.g., a cube $12 \times 12 \times 12$ or a square cross-section $24 \times 24 \times 6$), duplicate geometric orientations collapse so no redundant search states are evaluated.

### 2.5 Strict Grade Segregation
- The client facility handles ~80 distinct polyurethane foam grades (e.g., `HR-40`, `HR-30`, `VE-50`, `CM-28`).
- Buns, remnants, work orders, and parts belong to a single foam grade.
- **Parts of grade $G$ are cut strictly from stock (buns or remnants) of grade $G$.** Cross-grade nesting is prohibited.

### 2.6 Remnant Tracking & Aging Degradation
- Leftover rectangular pieces that exceed minimum salvageable dimensions (e.g., $\ge 18'' \times 18'' \times 12''$) are flagged as **reusable remnants** rather than unrecoverable scrap.
- **Oxidation Aging Rule:** Polyurethane remnants oxidize and discolor on shop floor racks. After 2–3 days of storage, a remnant must lose an additional **1 inch** on each face before reuse.
- The system models this aging function:
  $$\text{effective\_usable\_dim} = \max(0, \text{nominal\_dim} - 2 \times \text{aging\_trim}) \quad \text{if age} \ge 3 \text{ days}$$
- In the MVP, this function is calculated and displayed in the Remnant Inventory panel to give planners visibility into degrading offcuts.

### 2.7 Machine Fleet Profiles
- Each saw has a machine profile:
  - `name`: Identifier (e.g., "Horizontal Saw H-1", "Vertical Guillotine V-2").
  - `cut_type`: `guillotine` (implemented in MVP) or `free` (waterjet/CNC, registered as stub returning clear "not implemented" status).
  - `max_cut_height`: Maximum throat height the saw can pass (e.g., 42").
  - `kerf`: Specific kerf for this machine (e.g., 0.125").
  - `automation`: `manual`, `rotating`, or `CNC`.

## 3. The 3D Guillotine Slicing Tree (k-d Bounding Hierarchy)

To guarantee that any calculated 3D layout can **actually be cut on physical saws**, the layout is modeled as a **Recursive Guillotine Slicing Tree** (a 3D k-d tree).

```
                      [Root Usable Block (106 x 80 x 38)]
                                     |
                       === Cut 1: Axis Z at 12" === (Kerf = 0.125")
                                    / \
                                   /   \
        [Slab 1: 106 x 80 x 12]            [Slab 2: 106 x 80 x 25.875]
                 |                                      |
     === Cut 2: Axis X at 36" ===            === Cut 5: Axis Z at 16" ===
              /     \                                  /    \
   [Strip A]       [Strip B]               [Sub-slab 1]    [Remnant Box]
       |               |
  === Cut 3 ===   === Cut 4 ===
     /   \           /   \
 [Part] [Part]   [Part] [Scrap]
```

### 3.1 Properties of the Slicing Tree:
1. **Root Node:** Represents the post-trim usable bounding volume of the block.
2. **Internal Nodes (Cut Nodes):** Represent a guillotine cut through the entire current volume.
   - Specified by `(axis, position, kerf)`.
   - Divides the parent box into two child boxes: `left_child` and `right_child`.
3. **Leaf Nodes:** Terminal spaces that are either:
   - A **Placed Part** (with reference to Part SKU, order ID, exact 3D coordinates, and orientation).
   - A **Reusable Remnant** (an empty box meeting minimum salvage thresholds).
   - An **Unrecoverable Scrap Box** (an empty box too small to be reused).

By representing the nesting strictly as a slicing tree, **non-guillotine configurations (interlocking or impossible-to-saw shapes) are mathematically impossible to generate**.

## 4. Placement Strategy: Best-Fit Decreasing 3D Guillotine (BFD-G3D)

The core optimization engine executes an intelligent **Best-Fit Decreasing Guillotine Placement**:

### Step 1: Ordering and Sorting
- Group required parts by Foam Grade.
- For each grade, expand parts by quantity into individual part instances.
- Sort parts in **Best-Fit Decreasing order**:
  1. Primary: Volume descending ($\text{Length} \times \text{Width} \times \text{Height}$).
  2. Secondary: Maximum single dimension descending (places long or tall parts before smaller filler items).

### Step 2: Stock Selection (Remnants First, Then Full Buns)
- For the active foam grade, examine available stock:
  1. Usable active remnants from inventory (sorted by aging urgency and fit).
  2. Standard raw buns (e.g., 108" × 82" × 40").
- The engine opens stock sequentially, striving to satisfy parts from existing remnants before opening a fresh full bun.

### Step 3: Candidate Space Search & Orientation Trial
- Maintain a list of currently available **free rectangular spaces** in the active block's slicing tree.
- For the next part to place:
  - Generate all permitted 3D orientations based on the part's rise constraint (`rise-locked`, `free-in-plane`, `any`).
  - For each permitted orientation $(l, w, h)$:
    - For each free space box $(S_x, S_y, S_z)$:
      - Check fit: Is $l \le S_x$ and $w \le S_y$ and $h \le S_z$?
      - If it fits, compute the **waste score** (volume leftover, aspect ratio fit, and remaining remnant quality).
  - Select the `(free_space, orientation)` pair that minimizes unrecoverable scrap and preserves the largest contiguous residual block.

### Step 4: Recursive Guillotine Subdivision (Slicing)
When a part of dimensions $(l, w, h)$ is placed inside a free box $(S_x, S_y, S_z)$ at origin $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$:
- If dimensions match exactly along an axis, no cut is made along that axis.
- Otherwise, the residual space is partitioned into up to three orthogonal free boxes using guillotine cuts:
  - **Slab Cut (Z axis):** If $S_z > h + \text{kerf}$, cut at height $z = z_0 + h$, leaving a top residual box of dimensions $(S_x, S_y, S_z - h - \text{kerf})$.
  - **Rip Cut (X axis):** In the bottom slab of height $h$, if $S_x > l + \text{kerf}$, cut at length $x = x_0 + l$, leaving a lateral strip of dimensions $(S_x - l - \text{kerf}, S_y, h)$.
  - **Crosscut (Y axis):** In the remaining box of dimensions $(l, S_y, h)$, if $S_y > w + \text{kerf}$, cut at width $y = y_0 + w$, leaving an end offcut of dimensions $(l, S_y - w - \text{kerf}, h)$.
- The exact guillotine slicing order ($Z \to X \to Y$ or $X \to Y \to Z$) is chosen to maximize the largest single residual offcut (encouraging usable remnants over fragmented scrap).
- Each residual box is registered as a new candidate free space.

### Step 5: Multi-Block Continuation
- If parts remain that cannot fit into the current block, the current block is finalized (leftovers classified as either scrap or tagged remnants), and the next block/remnant is opened.

## 5. Cut Sequencer: Converting Slicing Trees into Saw-Operator Instructions

Saw operators do not look at raw k-d trees; they operate a physical saw cutting one piece at a time. The **Cut Sequencer** flattens the slicing tree into an intuitive, sequential step-by-step cutting roadmap:

```
Step 1: Raw Bun Skin Trim
        Remove 1.0" perimeter from all 6 faces of Bun #1 (108 x 82 x 40).
        Usable block size: 106.0" x 80.0" x 38.0".

Step 2: Horizontal Slab Cut (Z = 12.0")
        Place block on bed. Set saw fence / height to 12.00".
        Cut lengthwise along X. Kerf allowance: 1/8".
        Produces: Slab A (106 x 80 x 12) on bed, Slab B (106 x 80 x 25.875) top.

Step 3: Rip Cut on Slab A (X = 36.0")
        Fence: 36.00". Direction: Widthwise (Y).
        Produces: Strip 1 (36 x 80 x 12) and Strip 2 (69.875 x 80 x 12).

Step 4: Crosscut on Strip 1 (Y = 24.0")
        Produces: Finished Part Cushion A (36 x 24 x 12). Tag Part #4821-A1.

Step N: Tag Reusable Remnant
        Offcut 36" x 28" x 25.875" exceeds salvage threshold.
        Prompt: "KEEP OFFCUT: Tag Remnant #REM-4821-1 (Bay R-12)".
```

Every step generated contains:
- `step_number` and `total_steps`.
- `cut_type`: `skin_trim`, `horizontal_slab`, `vertical_rip`, `crosscut`.
- `axis`: `X`, `Y`, or `Z`.
- `position_in`: Dimension setting for saw fence / elevation.
- `kerf_in`: Blade width allowance.
- `target_piece`: Dimensions and description of workpiece on table.
- `output_pieces`: Resulting pieces (finished part with customer label, sub-slab, or offcut).
- `remnant_prompt`: Explicit alert if an offcut should be retained and barcoded.

## 6. Honest Metrics & Material Utilization Reporting

To build absolute trust with plant managers and machine operators, calculations are fully exposed and never rounded up to claim unrealistic yields:

$$\text{Gross Bun Volume} = L_{\text{nominal}} \times W_{\text{nominal}} \times H_{\text{nominal}}$$
$$\text{Skin Trim Loss} = \text{Gross Bun Volume} - (L_{\text{usable}} \times W_{\text{usable}} \times H_{\text{usable}})$$
$$\text{Total Parts Volume} = \sum_{p \in \text{Placed}} (L_p \times W_p \times H_p)$$
$$\text{Total Kerf Loss} = \sum_{c \in \text{Cuts}} (\text{Area of Cut Plane}_c \times \text{kerf})$$
$$\text{Usable Remnants Volume} = \sum_{r \in \text{Remnants}} (L_r \times W_r \times H_r)$$
$$\text{Unrecoverable Scrap} = \text{Gross Bun Volume} - \text{Total Parts Volume} - \text{Usable Remnants Volume} - \text{Skin Trim Loss} - \text{Total Kerf Loss}$$

The UI reports:
1. **Gross Material Yield:** $\frac{\text{Total Parts Volume}}{\text{Gross Bun Volume}} \times 100\%$
2. **Net Usable Yield:** $\frac{\text{Total Parts Volume}}{\text{Usable Interior Volume}} \times 100\%$
3. **Total Material Recovery (Parts + Remnants):** $\frac{\text{Total Parts Volume} + \text{Usable Remnants Volume}}{\text{Gross Bun Volume}} \times 100\%$
4. **Estimated Cutting Time:** Estimated from total linear cut inches and machine speed.
